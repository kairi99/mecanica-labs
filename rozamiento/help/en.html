Theory of friction

When two surfaces in contact slide, or are about to, a force appears parallel to the surface that opposes the relative motion. While the body is at rest this static force adjusts to whatever is needed, up to a maximum. Once the body slides, the kinetic force has an almost fixed value. In the Amontons-Coulomb model both are proportional to the normal force N and do not depend on the contact area:

fs ≤ μs·N
fk = μk·N

The coefficients μs and μk are dimensionless numbers that depend on both surfaces in contact, not on one. It is common for μs to be larger than μk, and that is one of the questions your data have to answer.

Block on a horizontal table (experiments 1 and 2)

Draw the free-body diagram of the block: the weight W = (M + m)·g down, the normal N up, the spring-scale force F horizontal and friction opposing it. Horizontally there is no acceleration when the block is about to move or moves at constant speed, and vertically N = W. So:

Fmax = μ·M·g   (about to move, no added masses)
F = μ·(M + m)·g   (constant speed)

If you plot F against the weight W you get a straight line. What its slope and its intercept represent you work out yourself from your diagram (tasks 1 and 3).

Bar on a raised rail (experiment 3)

On a plane inclined by an angle θ the weight has a component mg·sin θ along the plane and mg·cos θ perpendicular to it, which is the normal. The bar stays at rest while mg·sin θ ≤ μs·mg·cos θ, that is, while tan θ ≤ μs. Once it slides:

m·a = m·g·sin θ − μ·m·g·cos θ
μ = tan θ − a / (g·cos θ)

The mass cancels, so the bar's mass does not enter the result. If the bar starts from rest and the acceleration is constant, a = 2·d/t².

Using the lab

The lab goes in steps, which you see in the bar above: 1 Assembly, 2 Experiment 1, 3 Experiment 2, 4 Experiment 3, 5 Reference record, 6 Analysis. With ?reference=0 or ?analysis=0 in the address, steps 5 and 6 are hidden. The "Procedure" follows the guide and, with "Show me", takes you to the panel you need.

Materials the guide does not list. The guide's materials list does not mention the rack-and-pinion stand and its base, the stopwatch or the balance. But the procedure needs them: the stand to raise the rail and the stopwatch to time the travel (experiment 3), and the balance to measure the block's mass M. In the simulator you find them in the inventory box, along with a few items that are of no use in this practice.
  1. Assembly. Put what you need on the table. Weigh the block: you get M with the balance's resolution.
  2. Experiment 1 (force at constant speed). Fix the paper to the table, rest one face of the block, choose the spring scale and zero it lying flat. Pull at constant speed and press "Read" when the needle holds steady. Five readings per face and per added mass (0, 50, 100 and 150 g).
  3. Experiment 2 (largest force). With no masses, pull slowly and press "Read" at the instant of the largest force, just before the block starts. Afterwards the needle drops.
  4. Experiment 3 (rail). Measure and mark 100 cm on the rail (with the bar on the start mark it fits on the 1.2 m rail), put a bar, turn the stand's crank until it slides without stopping and stop it at once. Measure h with the tape and time the travel. Five tries per bar.
  5. Analysis. It computes from your tables and updates by itself. The answers in words are yours; the comparison with the literature uses the value and source you type.

Which pull speed to use

With a soft spring and static friction larger than kinetic, the block can advance in jumps (stick-slip): it sticks, the spring stretches to the maximum, it lets go, relaxes and sticks again. With slow pulls the needle goes up and down with no steady reading. In this simulator, with a steady hand, the motion becomes continuous from about 1 to 3 cm/s depending on the load (minimum: 1.0 cm/s with sandpaper and 150 g on the 1 N scale; 3.0 cm/s with cloth and masses on the 2 N one). Pull at 4 cm/s or more in experiment 1: the needle settles and about 4 s of steady reading remain on the sheet, for any load. In experiment 2, on the other hand, pull slowly (below 1 cm/s) to have time to react. A higher speed raises the kinetic force a little (friction increases slightly with speed in this model) and lowers the maximum force the needle shows, because of the spring scale's own damping.

The A4 sheet is about 30 cm long; the block takes part of it and leaves a useful travel of about 20 cm. If it leaves the sheet, friction changes (it rubs on the table).

The spring scale

It is a spring with a needle over a printed scale. By Hooke's law the needle moves in proportion to the force: F = k·x. There are two, 1 N (0.02 N marks) and 2 N (0.05 N marks); the 2 N one is stiffer and has coarser marks.

  • Full scale. If the real force exceeds the maximum, the needle sits on the stop and the reading is not valid. With sandpaper and the heaviest masses the 1 N one falls short: use the 2 N. With cloth the 1 N is enough.
  • Zero. It comes set with the scale hanging. Lying flat, the spring no longer supports the weight of the moving parts and the needle reads low by a small, fixed amount (a few hundredths of a newton). Turn the zero screw with the scale lying flat. If you do not, the error shows up as a non-zero intercept in your line.
  • Your press. "Read" notes the needle at the exact instant you press, not at the best instant. The largest force is a short peak: if you are late, you read a smaller force.
  • Reading. You read the nearest mark. The Type B uncertainty of each reading is the division divided by √12.

The raised rail and its geometry

The 1.2 m rail rests on the table on the lower edge of its low end, and the rack-and-pinion stand holds it 1.10 m from that point, measured along the rail. The height h is that of the rail's lower edge at the clamp above the table. With that definition the sine of the angle is exact:

sin θ = h / L,   L = 1.10 m

The bar leaves the start mark (its front edge on the mark) and covers the distance you marked, 100 cm in the guide. If you keep turning the crank while the bar slides, the angle keeps growing and the acceleration is not constant; the simulator represents it as it is. If you stop it, the angle stays fixed.

What you actually measure

The height h at which the bar starts sliding and the time it takes to cover the rail need not depend on the same coefficient. Think about which physical condition sets each one: deciding whether your μ is static or kinetic is task 6. Right at the start the acceleration is small and the times scatter a lot.

Uncertainty

  • Mean forces. Type A: s/√n of the five readings. Type B: the spring scale's division divided by √12 (combined in quadrature; Type B does not shrink with repetition). The result is F̄ ± ΔF.
  • F against W line. Weighted fit with weights 1/(ΔF)². If the fit is poor, the slope's uncertainty is widened by √(reduced χ²). The uncertainties of W, a few hundredths of a newton, are negligible next to ΔF.
  • μ = F̄max/(M·g). The uncertainties of F̄max, of the mass (balance) and of g are propagated.
  • Experiment 3. Each try propagates the time uncertainty (0.01 s display and a reaction bias of ±0.1 s) and that of h (1 mm tape and your judgment of the instant sliding starts), and L and d are added. The result is μ̄ ± δμ per material.
  • Comparison. Type the literature value and the source. The app computes the relative error and compares it with your uncertainty; it does not give you the value.

Error sources you will see in your data

  • Loose paper: the spring scale measures the friction of the paper on the table. Low, erratic readings.
  • Spring scale not zeroed horizontally: all forces shifted by the same amount.
  • Slow pull and soft spring: the needle jumps (stick-slip).
  • Reading after the start of motion: the largest force comes out low and μs may look smaller than μk.
  • 1 N spring scale at its stop: the reading is not the force.
  • Nominal 50 g masses: each differs a little from its label; if you weigh them on the balance you will notice.
  • Crank stopped late: the height you read is above that at the start of sliding.
  • Hand timing of a 1.5 s travel: a 0.1 s delay is 7 % of the time and 13 % of the acceleration.
  • Reading h with the tape while the crank turns: not possible; stop, then measure.

An honest model

The simulator does not reproduce the coefficients of the real material: they are assumptions, documented in PARAMETERS.md with their plausibility range. For the block, the spring scale and the paper, the equation of the spring with static friction up to the start and kinetic afterwards is solved, with a small speed dependence and the spring scale's damping; that is why you see the peak, the drop and, at low speed, stick-slip. For the bar the condition tan θ ≥ μs is applied with different values in each try (the surface is never the same). The "Reference record" shows you the true force against time and the true height and time, so you can compare with what you read.

What the model lacks: table tilt, pull angle (always horizontal), tipping of the block or the bars, and friction varying with area.

Accessibility

This lab was checked with the automated tool axe-core (rules of the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.0, levels A and AA) on the lab page, in Spanish and English, in light and dark theme, at phone and desktop widths. An automated tool does not find every problem. No manual tests of this practice have been done yet (keyboard-only use, hand-measured contrast, 200 % zoom, a 320 px width, "reduce motion") and none with a screen reader.

  • Keyboard use is designed with the same shared components as the other practices: the first link of each page skips to the main content and Esc closes menus, dialogs and the tour and returns focus. This has not yet been tested by hand with a keyboard.
  • The assistant's hints and the refusals are announced in a status region and do not disappear by themselves; they are closed with their button.
  • Colours are never the only means: every state also has text or a mark.

Known limits

  • The 3D view is visual. Every action has an equivalent control in the panels (buttons and sliders), and the 2D view and the panels show the same information in text.
  • The spring scale's needle is a drawing; its value is noted with "Read", which also announces it in text.
  • Timing and reading the needle are timed tasks by design: measuring with your own press is the experiment. No other part has a time limit.

How to report a problem

If you find an accessibility barrier, open an issue on the project's issues page and say the page, the device and the assistive technology you use.
