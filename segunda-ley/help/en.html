Theory: Newton's second law for the DigiCart–canister system

The DigiCart (mass Mc with everything it carries) is tied by a string over a pulley to a hanging canister of mass m. If the string is ideal and there is no friction, both bodies have the same acceleration a and the second law, applied to each, gives

m g − T = m a    and    T = Mc a  ⇒  m g = (Mc + m) a = Mt a.

Here F = m g is the weight of the canister with its masses (what the dynamometer reads) and Mt is the total mass being accelerated. That is why, in each trial, a 10 g mass moves from the canister to the DigiCart: the force goes down but the total mass hardly changes, and the graph of F against a is a straight line whose slope is Mt in the ideal case.

Starting from rest with constant acceleration, the distance d = 90 cm is covered in a time t with d = ½ a t2, so a = 2d/t2. That is the acceleration the analysis computes for each force.

What the ideal case leaves out

  • Rotational inertia. The wheels of the DigiCart and the pulley roll and spin as they move; their rotational kinetic energy behaves like an extra mass (ΣI/r2 of the wheels and Ip/rp2 of the pulley). The string has mass too. The effective mass is larger than Mt, and the slope of the line approaches it.
  • Friction. Rolling resistance, the wheel bearings and the pulley axle take an almost constant force away from F. The line of F against a does not go through the origin: F = M a + F0, with F0 positive. The slope is the mass that the force accelerates; the intercept is the part of the force that produces no acceleration.
  • Residual tilt. A "horizontal" rail with a centred bubble can still have a very small tilt: the weight of the DigiCart adds or subtracts Mc g sin θ from the force, and that moves F0.
  • The plastic screws. Every mass that moves to the DigiCart brings its plastic screw (0.8 g in this model, assumed): the total mass grows a little in each trial, just as the force goes down. It is a real, small effect that changes the slope and the intercept of the fit; it is not an app error.
  • The weight of the hanging string also pulls on the system (a little more at the end of the run).

How to use the lab

  1. Assembly. All the materials are loose on the table. Drag them to their place to make the connections: stand on its base, rail in the stand, receiver with pulley on the other end, DigiCart on the rail, string tied to the DigiCart with the brass screw, over the pulley and tied to the canister. The table holds things this practice does not use (a 50 g mass, a marble, a two-leg stand).
  2. Settings. Level the rail (the height of the stand end in mm and the fine screw of the receiver), stick the start mark and the 90 cm mark, put the DigiCart with its front on the start mark and choose the string length: long enough to reach the pulley, short enough that the canister does not touch the floor before 90 cm. The four masses start loose on the table: drag them into the canister or onto the DigiCart.
  3. Measurements. Weigh the system with the balance and record Mt. Hang the canister from the dynamometer and record F in Table 3.1. Release the DigiCart with S, start the stopwatch with Space and stop it when the front passes the 90 cm mark; Enter copies the time. Repeat 10 times. Move a mass from the canister to the DigiCart (with its plastic screw), read F again and repeat with 3, 2 and 1 masses in the canister (an optional fifth row uses the empty canister).
  4. DigiCart log (hidden with ?reference=0). The position of the DigiCart during each run, measured by its own sensor, and a comparison with your stopwatch.
  5. Analysis. Mean time with its uncertainty, acceleration with its uncertainty, graph of F against a with the fitted line, fitted mass against Mt and percent error; export a CSV. The explanation questions of the guide are yours to answer: the app does not answer them.

Assembling by dragging

At the start (and after “Start over”) every part is loose on the table and the track is not built. Drag a part to its place: the valid spots light up with an outline and a label, and a part dropped near one snaps there. The spots are: the stand on its base, the rail in the stand clamp (by its 0 mm end or its 1200 mm end), the receiver with pulley on the other end of the rail, the DigiCart on the rail, the brass screw on the DigiCart, the string over the pulley, the canister tied to the string, the 10 g masses inside the canister or on the DigiCart (one plastic screw each: there are five) and the level on the rail. Moving a mass from the canister to the DigiCart is exactly the guide’s procedure: drag the mass from one place to the other. If the connection cannot be made yet (for example the rail before the stand is on its base), the part stays where it was and the reason is shown: trying things in the wrong order teaches too. You can drag a part that is already in place to another spot, or drop it on the tray (under the stage) to take it off the table; it waits there until you drag it back.

Without dragging: open “Pick a part on the table (n)” under the stage (or use a part from the tray), tap the part (or focus it with Tab and press Enter) and then tap a highlighted spot, or choose the spot in the list that appears under the tray (arrows and Enter). Esc cancels. If you prefer buttons, the “Inventory and assembly” panel keeps them under “Assemble with buttons”; they do exactly the same.

If the DigiCart runs off the rail

With no receiver and pulley at the end of the rail nothing stops the DigiCart. If you release it like that, the rail (held only by the stand) droops a little toward its free end, the DigiCart rolls along it, passes the end and falls to the floor: it flies like a projectile (with the Quito g), bounces a little and slides to a stop. With reduced motion set in the system (prefers-reduced-motion) it jumps straight to its final position. That release is flagged as “not valid”: it has no end-mark time and no DigiCart record, and it does not count for Table 3.1. To go on, put the DigiCart back on the rail (drag it, or use “Back to start”) and fit the receiver before releasing again. The values of the fall (bounce, floor friction, droop of the rail) are assumptions documented in PARAMETERS.md.

A note on the guide: the procedure asks to measure the weight of the canister with a dynamometer, which is not in its materials list. The virtual lab offers two (1 N and 2 N) and the balance.

If something goes wrong, the simulator shows it as it would happen on the bench: a string that is too long leaves the canister on the floor before 90 cm (the string goes slack and the DigiCart coasts on, slowing down); too short, it does not reach the pulley; without the brass screw nothing pulls the DigiCart; taking a mass out of the system changes the total mass.

The instruments

  • Digital balance, 0.1 g resolution (assumed). Type B uncertainty of a reading: 0.1 g/√12 = 0.029 g.
  • Spring dynamometer, read to the nearest division: 0.02 N on the 1 N one and 0.05 N on the 2 N one (assumed). It is used hanging, with its zero set hanging, so it has no zero error. Type B uncertainty: division/√12.
  • Digital stopwatch, 0.01 s; the displayed time is truncated. You start and stop with your own reaction (about 0.2 s), which adds scatter and a possible bias.
  • Printed rail scale, in millimetres. For the 90 cm between two marks there are two readings: u = √2 · 1 mm/√12 = 0.41 mm.

Uncertainty

The GUM (JCGM 100) and J. R. Taylor's book are followed. For each force:

  • The mean time of the n readings, with type A uncertainty s/√n, type B of the resolution (0.01 s/√12) and type B of the reaction bias (assumed half-width of 0.1 s, rectangular), combined in quadrature: that is the Δt shown.
  • The acceleration a = 2d/t2, with Δa propagated: (Δa/a)2 = (Δd/d)2 + (2Δt/t)2. Only the statistical part of Δt enters the weights of the fit; the reaction bias is common to all readings and does not average out, so it is shown apart (how much the fitted mass would change).
  • The weighted least-squares fit F = Mfit a + F0 by the effective-variance method (Orear): the uncertainty of a is carried over to F multiplied by the slope. It gives the slope, the intercept, their uncertainties and the reduced χ².
  • The comparison with Mt: percent error and the difference in units of the combined uncertainty.

With 4 points and 2 parameters there are only 2 degrees of freedom: the reduced χ² is very variable and a single pair of points can pull the line. Do not read it as a test but as a warning.

Error sources the simulation contains

  • Reaction time and rounding of the stopwatch (the dominant error: with times of 1 to 2.5 s, a 0.1 s bias changes the fitted mass by tens of percent).
  • Rotational inertia of the wheels, the pulley and the string; rolling, bearing and axle friction; air drag.
  • Residual tilt of the rail and the effect of the level.
  • Tolerance of the masses and the canister (±0.1 g and ±0.3 g, assumed) and the plastic screws.
  • Resolution of the balance and of the dynamometer.
  • Releasing with a small push or off the mark; the weight of the string.
  • The canister hitting the floor, the DigiCart reaching the receiver, a badly tied string: set-up errors that change the experiment.

How to reduce these errors and which ones dominate is part of what the guide asks you to answer (tasks 7 and 8).

An honest model

The masses, the resistances and the bench heights are assumptions documented with value, unit and date in PARAMETERS.md (Practice 3 section); none is a PHYWE specification and none was tuned to make the result "come out right". The equations: mhang g + Mc g sin θ − Fres(v) = Meff a, with rolling inertia and the weight of the string included; after the canister hits the floor the string goes slack and the DigiCart slows down by friction alone.

The acceleration of the model is lower than F/Mt, and the fitted line is not exactly the ideal one. If your data do not match the ideal, that is information, not a failure.

Accessibility

This lab was checked with the automated tool axe-core (rules of the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.0, levels A and AA) on the lab page, in Spanish and English, in light and dark theme, at phone and desktop widths. An automated tool does not find every problem. No manual tests of this practice have been done yet (keyboard-only use, hand-measured contrast, 200 % zoom, a 320 px width, "reduce motion") and none with a screen reader.

  • Keyboard use is designed with the same shared components as the other practices: the first link of each page skips to the main content and Esc closes menus, dialogs and the tour and returns focus. This has not yet been tested by hand with a keyboard.
  • Coach hints and refusals are announced through a status region and do not disappear on their own; you close them with their button.
  • Colour is never the only cue: every state also has text or a mark.

Known limits

  • The 3D view is visual. Everything done in it has an equivalent control in the panels (number fields, sliders and buttons), and the 2D side view and the panels give the same information as text.
  • The charts have a name and a short description; their values are in the tables and the text next to them.
  • Timing is a timed task by design: measuring a time with your own presses is the experiment. Nothing else has a time limit.

How to report a problem

If you find an accessibility barrier, open an issue on the project's issues page and tell us the page, the device and the assistive technology you use.
